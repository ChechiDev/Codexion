## Description

`int wait_for_dongles(t_coder *coder)` in
`coders/src/dongle/wait_for_dongles.c`: one bounded wait of a coder whose
request is queued in both dongle heaps but cannot take them yet. 6.3.4 calls it
in its loop after checking the stop and the readiness of both dongles.

**Precondition.** The caller holds both dongle mutexes of `coder`, locked lower
index first (`first_dongle` then `second_dongle`, 5.1.1), and has just checked
`is_stopped` while holding them (so a stop broadcast cannot be lost: the first
mutex stays held from that check until the cond wait releases it, 6.3.1).

**Wait.** `pthread_cond_timedwait` releases one mutex only, so the second
mutex is unlocked first and the thread waits on the **first** dongle's cond
with a 1 ms deadline, then relocks the second (it already holds the first,
so the lower-index-first order holds). A change signalled on the first
dongle's cond (release 6.3.3, stop 6.3.1) wakes it at once; a change on the
second dongle is seen at the latest when the 1 ms slice ends. The thread is
blocked in the cond in between, so there is no busy-wait (PLAN.md "Dongle").
The extra latency (at most 1 ms plus scheduling per wait) is part of the
PLAN.md `margin` of the viability bound. A remaining cooldown is always at
least 1 ms when it blocks (integer ms), so a fixed 1 ms slice is never longer
than needed.

**Return contract.** 0 after a wake-up or a timeout, holding both mutexes; -1
on invalid input (no lock touched) or on a runtime failure (deadline, timed
wait error other than `ETIMEDOUT`, relock), holding nothing, so the caller goes
straight to its fatal path (6.3.4: `set_stopped` + `wake_dongles`).

**Name.** `wait_` blocks until a condition holds; `wait_for_dongles` is the
example of `docs/conventions.md`. Checked: no `wait_for_dongles` helper exists
in `tests/`.

Orchestration (PLAN.md layers: `dongle` module, uses the `time` adapter).
Close-time documentation: the `dongle/` row of `docs/architecture.md` explains
the first-cond wait and the 1 ms slice.
